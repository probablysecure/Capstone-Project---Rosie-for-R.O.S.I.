How to estimate ROI assumptions

Use organization-specific evidence where available. STIG severity does not determine these financial inputs.

What the estimate means

These values are planning assumptions, not predictions or guaranteed savings.

Expected benefit per year = annual incident probability × cost per incident × expected risk reduction

For a multi-year estimate: ROI = (expected benefit over the period − implementation and recurring costs) ÷ those costs. Enter percentages as percent values, such as 10 for 10%.

1. Annual incident probability before fix

Estimate the chance that the relevant incident scenario will occur in the next 12 months if this finding remains unresolved. This is an organization and system specific estimate; it is not derived from CAT severity.

  • Start with incidents, near misses, and security events for similar systems over several years.
  • Adjust for the system's exposure, data sensitivity, threat activity, and existing safeguards.
  • If you have event counts, estimate the rate per system-year. Under a steady, independent-event assumption, convert that rate λ to annual probability with 1 − e−λ. Sparse history means substantial uncertainty; zero observed incidents does not prove zero risk.

Example: 1 comparable event in 10 system-years gives λ = 0.1 and an estimated annual probability of about 9.5%. A 10% input is roughly a 1-in-10 chance over one year.

2. Cost per incident

Estimate the total cost if that scenario occurs. Match the cost boundary to your scenario and planning period so you do not count the same loss twice.

  • Include response and recovery labor, restoration, downtime, and outside support.
  • Consider business interruption, customer impact, and other material consequences that apply to your organization.
  • Use prior incident costs, finance estimates, insurance analyses, or a documented range. Avoid treating maximum theoretical penalties as the likely cost.

Example: if response, restoration, and lost operations are estimated at $100,000 combined, enter 100000.

3. Expected risk reduction from this fix

Estimate how much of the expected loss the specific remediation would reduce if implemented successfully. This is not the STIG's CAT score and should not automatically be set to 100%.

  • Consider how directly the fix addresses the incident path or weakness.
  • Account for coverage, deployment quality, user adoption, remaining attack paths, and compensating controls.
  • Ask the technical owner or risk team for a defensible estimate; document why and use a conservative value when evidence is limited.

Example: 40% means you estimate the fix will reduce the scenario's expected loss by about 40%, leaving residual risk.

4. Cost to implement this fix

Estimate the one-time cost to complete and validate the remediation.

  • Include staff time using a loaded labor rate, including planning, implementation, testing, and change management.
  • Add one-time vendor, professional service, hardware, or deployment costs.
  • Put ongoing licenses, support, and maintenance in the separate recurring annual cost field.

Example: 20 staff hours at $100/hour plus $500 in one-time services gives a $2,500 implementation estimate.

Worked example

At 10% annual probability, $100,000 cost per incident, and 40% expected risk reduction, the estimated benefit is $4,000 per year. If implementation costs $2,500 and recurring cost is $500 per year, the one-year investment is $3,000 and the simple one-year ROI is about 33%.

About Rosie

A project to make security remediation easier to plan.

Why I created this

I created Rosie because reviewing STIG findings can leave teams with a long list of fixes, but not an easy way to compare what those fixes may cost or how they could reduce risk. I wanted a straightforward place to explore findings, make the assumptions behind an ROI estimate visible, and organize potential fixes by quarter or technology.

This project is a work in progress. The estimates depend on the assumptions entered by the user; they are meant to support planning and discussion, not replace security, financial, or risk-team judgment.

Independent project

Rosie is an independent project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by DISA or STIG Viewer. Checklist files and saved collections stay in this browser on this device in this prototype.
